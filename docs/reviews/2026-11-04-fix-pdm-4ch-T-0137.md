## The question for you, in plain words

**What happens.** A negative spike on the battery input while the capacitors on VS are charged puts more voltage across Q30 than its 60 V rating. A TVS across Q30 solves that. But the TVS keeps conducting until its voltage has fallen back to its breakdown voltage, while D30 holds the input negative. So VS ends at about (TVS breakdown voltage − D30 voltage). If D30 is holding a higher voltage than the TVS breakdown, **VS, and so C31, goes below 0 V** for the rest of the spike.

C31 is the capacitor decision 82 was meant to protect. Its datasheet gives no reverse rating, and general practice is about 1 V at most for a short time. The other parts on VS cope: C104–C404 are non-polar, the output switches are rated for reverse, and R60 limits the current to IO10 to under 70 µA.

**Why it cannot be avoided completely.** The two limits pull in opposite directions. A low-voltage TVS keeps Q30 safe but takes VS further negative. A high-voltage TVS keeps VS positive but goes over 60 V. At 31 A, VS ≥ 0 needs a TVS breakdown of at least 48.4 V, and 5 % parts in that range reach 60 V or more at 31 A. The nearest are below.

**How big the risk is.** VS only goes negative if the spike can drive more than about 20 A into the board and lasts more than about 150 µs. The textbook pulse (ISO 7637-2 pulse 1, about 12–15 A) keeps VS positive with the 40 V parts. Whether such spikes occur on a real boat's bus at all is still unverified (T-0130). It could be checked on a prototype: with the battery off, apply a pulse generator to J10 and scope VS, VBAT_IN and VDS.

## Point 2 – pulse current basis: accepted, with one correction

**The reasoning holds.** The TVS current ends in VBAT_IN, so it cannot exceed what the source pulls from that node, and D30 has 48.4 V only up to its Ipp of 31.0 A (facts file).

**Correction.** "At most D30's Ipp" holds exactly only for a source that behaves like a current source, such as an inductor. A voltage source with resistance drives more at the start, because VBAT_IN is less negative while the TVS conducts and D30 has not started. The right bound is the source's **short-circuit current** Us/Ri, not the current it drives into D30's clamp.

So the assumption I used is: **the source's short-circuit current is at most 31 A.** A source tuned to give exactly 31 A at 48.4 V has a higher short-circuit current:

| Us | Ri | Short-circuit current |
|---|---|---|
| 600 V | 17.8 Ω | 34 A |
| 100 V | 1.66 Ω | 60 A |
| 60 V | 0.37 Ω | 160 A |

- With such a source, 5.0SMDJ40CA stays under 60 V for Us ≥ about 71 V.
- SMDJ40CA stays under 60 V only for Us ≥ about 334 V.
- An inductive load on the bus is limited by its own running current, so it fits the current-source model.

## Point 3 – checks per candidate

Method: a piecewise-linear TVS and D30, C = 109.2 µF nominal, stepped in time. Worst corners:
- VDS: TVS at VBR max.
- VS: TVS at VBR min, D30 at VBR max.

VS at the end = VBR min − (36.8 V + 0.374 Ω × I). This is a floor that does not depend on C.

**(a) VDS below 60 V at 31 A:** see the table above. SMCJ30CA, SMCJ33CA, SMDJ40CA and 5.0SMDJ40CA pass, with margins of 11.6, 5.4, 0.6 and 4.7 V. SMCJ36, 40 and 43 fail.

The margins are for a cold TVS. VBR rises by 0.1 %/K (hongjiacheng sheet; the BORN sheet gives none), so SMDJ40CA reaches 60.4 V with the TVS at 47 °C. Q30's own 60 V is a 25 °C minimum and rises too, to about 63 V at 125 °C (T-0132). That cancels part of this, and the excess is small.

**(b) Reversed battery ≤ 28 V: passes for all.** VRWM is ≥ 30 V, so there is no conduction. Leakage is at most the IR limit, and into the R60 divider (242 k) it gives:

| Part | IR max | VS at most |
|---|---|---|
| SMCJ | 1 µA | −0.24 V |
| SMDJ40CA | 2 µA | −0.48 V |
| 5.0SMDJ40CA | 5 µA | −1.2 V |

These are upper bounds: IR is specified at VRWM (40 V), not 28 V, and only at 25 °C (hot IR is unverified). Harmless, so the "no time limit" claim can stay. Q30's IDSS is already there as well.

With VS charged at the moment of reversal (29 V + 28 V = 57 V across Q30), the TVS conducts once until VS = VBR − 28 V:
- SMCJ30CA: +5 to +9 V.
- 40 V parts: +16 to +21 V.

VDS is at most 57 V and VS does not go negative. Above D30's VBR (about 33 V) D30 conducts and the battery fuse clears, as before.

**(c) Normal operation: passes for all.** VDS is about 0 with Q30 on and about −1 V at power-up through the body diode. A bidirectional part does not conduct at either.

**(d) Energy and pulse rating.** These are for a 31 A source held for 1 ms, the longest time on the datasheet curves (my assumption, because a pulse length has no natural limit in this model).

"×" is the Fig. 1 reading at the equivalent pulse length divided by peak power, at 25 °C. Multiply by 0.6 for a TVS at 75 °C (Fig. 2).

- The energy is about twice the 80–115 mJ in the ruling, as the plan expected.
- On the SMCJ sheet, Fig. 1 (about 0.8 kW at 1 ms) contradicts the 1.5 kW at 10/1000 µs in the table. I used the lower curve. By the table, the SMCJ parts would pass (about 2 J).
- **A correction to the brief's load model.** The TVS does not feed the loads while VS is positive, because both draw from the capacitors. A load therefore shortens the TVS's work. It adds energy only once VS is negative. Then current flows from GND through the load and the output switch's body diode into VS and through the TVS for the rest of the pulse. The bound of source current × Vc × time remains valid. The load also makes VS less negative: −2.4 V instead of −4.0 V for the 40 V parts.
- Stop (iv) does not apply: SMDJ40CA and 5.0SMDJ40CA have ample rating.
