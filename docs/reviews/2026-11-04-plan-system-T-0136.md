# Plan T-0136 (2026-11-04): TVS across Q30 on pdm-4ch

**Result:** one package, nothing deferred, one decision row. The single item is T-0129, with your ruling Q-0066. No
board is ordered, and pdm-hc2 is not touched because it has no Q30.

## Package

**P1 – TVS across Q30 (T-0129), scope `pdm-4ch`.** Fit one TVS from Q30 drain (VS) to Q30 source (VBAT_FL) on the root
sheet, then correct design-notes §8.6 (b) and (c). `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` and its invariants do not change: invariant 4
still holds, because the OUT → VS → Q30 → D30 surge path stays. The first candidate is SMCJ30CA C19077607, already on the
BOM as D30, so it adds no new unique part. The package decides the final part, because of the three points below.

## Three points the ruling did not cover

The package checks these, and they may send it back to you. Datasheet figures for the TVS parts below are the usual
SMCJ family values from memory, **not yet checked**. The package reads the real ones first.

**1. The energy is higher than the 80–115 mJ in the ruling.** That figure was worked out for Q30 avalanching at 60–66 V.
A TVS clamps lower, so it takes more out of the capacitors, because VS has further to fall before the voltage across
Q30 is back at the clamp level. With E = C × (VS + 48.4 − Vc) × Vc, C = 109.2 µF and VS = 29 V:

| Clamp Vc | Energy from the capacitors | VS at the end |
|---|---|---|
| 66 V (Q30 avalanche, T-0132) | 82 mJ | +17.6 V |
| 60 V (Q30 avalanche, T-0132) | 114 mJ | +11.6 V |
| 50 V | 150 mJ | +1.6 V |
| 44 V | 160 mJ | −4.4 V |
| 38.7 V (the maximum of the formula) | 164 mJ | −9.7 V |

A load adds to this. After the capacitors have fallen, the TVS keeps feeding the loads for the rest of the pulse, and
this is where the 32 A case matters. These energies are still small for a 1500 W SMC part, so this should change the
sums and not the result.

**2. VS can go below 0 V during the pulse, which is new.** Today Q30 holds about 60–66 V, so VS stays positive during a
negative transient. VS ends at (voltage across the TVS) − (voltage across D30). If the TVS clamps lower than D30, a long
enough pulse pulls VS negative. With SMCJ30CA in both places, the worst tolerance (D30 at 48.4 V, the new TVS at 33.3 V)
gives about −15 V. It takes about 5 mC to move VS that far, roughly 150 µs at 31 A, so a pulse in the millisecond range
gets there. C31 is the part decision 82 was meant to protect from reverse voltage, and it has no reverse rating. So the
part with the lowest clamp is not automatically the right one.

A higher-voltage TVS avoids this, but its clamp is closer to 60 V, so it depends on point 3:

| TVS (family values, unchecked) | VBR | VC at Ipp | Largest current with VDS < 60 V (linear estimate) | VS at the end, worst tolerance, at that current |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SMCJ30CA (on the BOM) | 33.3–36.8 V | 48.4 V at 31 A | above its 31 A rating | −15 V at 31 A |
| SMCJ36CA | 40–44.2 V | 58.1 V at 25.8 A | about 29 A | about −8 V |
| SMCJ40CA | 44.4–49.1 V | 64.5 V at 23.3 A | about 16 A | about +1 V |
| SMCJ43CA | 47.8–52.8 V | 69.4 V at 21.6 A | about 9 A | about +7 V |

**3. The "pulse current" is not the 32 A load, and it decides the choice.** Two models give very different answers:
- **Stiff clamp (the T-0132 model):** VBAT_IN is held at −48.4 V whatever the current, and only C31's ESR limits the
  discharge. For SMCJ30CA this gives about 100 A and 74 V at 30 mΩ, and about 60 A and 59 V at 300 mΩ. C31's ESR has no
  lower limit in its facts file. On this model it fails at low ESR.
- **Real source:** the TVS current ends up in VBAT_IN, so it cannot exceed what the transient source pulls from that
  node. If it tried to, VBAT_IN would rise and D30 would stop conducting. D30's own 48.4 V only holds up to its 31 A
  rating, so "D30 clamps at −48.4 V" already means a source of at most about 31 A. On this model the current is at most
  31 A. For the textbook pulse (ISO 7637-2 pulse 1, about 12–15 A, from memory and to be checked), SMCJ40CA would meet
  both VDS < 60 V and VS ≥ 0 V.

The package uses the real-source model, states it as an assumption in the design notes and gives the stiff-clamp figure
next to it. T-0130 was closed on an assumed default, so whether such transients occur on a real boat's bus is still unverified. Your
ruling fits the TVS anyway.

**How this might come back to you:** if no part meets VDS < 60 V and also keeps VS from going below 0 V at the current
the package takes as its basis, it stops and asks you. The question would be whether you accept a short negative VS or
want something else. The stop conditions are in the brief.

## Deferred

Nothing. Measuring C31's ESR on the prototype was only an option in the question. Your ruling does not ask for it, so it
is not planned.

## Decisions

One row for Q-0066, amending decision 88 (**auto**, T-0121), which says "no TVS on VS". The TVS is across Q30, not from
VS to GND, and the surge path of invariant 4 stays. The part itself becomes an **auto** row from the board-fixer.

Decision 82 is not amended: the TVS adds to the blocker and does not replace it.

**Safety:** F4, the panel supply for navigation lights and bilge forced run, hangs on VS behind this circuit. The package
extends the §8.6 failure-mode note, and a competent person should review the circuit before installation.
